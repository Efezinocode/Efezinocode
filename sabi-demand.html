Featured build

Sabi Demand.

An AI-powered market-demand and business-opportunity research tool I'm building for aspiring entrepreneurs and small sellers.

BUILT BY EFEZINO GREAT UZEZI

Turn business questions into research.

Starting a business often begins with hard questions: what could I sell, where might there be demand, how should I think about prices, and who else is already selling it? I created Sabi Demand to help people explore those questions with their budget and location in mind.

The goal is to organize market research into a clearer starting point for decisions, not to promise sales or guaranteed profit.

Case study

The idea and how I built it.

Like PassSabi AI, Sabi Demand grew from a problem I wanted to explore into a working product that I keep testing and improving.

01

What gave me the idea

I kept thinking about people who want to make money or start a small business but don't know what to sell, where to start, or how to judge demand. I wanted to build a tool that could help turn those early questions into structured research.

02

Start with budget and location

I shaped the idea around two practical inputs: how much a person can spend and where they plan to do business. That gives the research a clearer context than a generic list of business ideas.

03

Map the questions a seller needs answered

I planned the research around products to consider, local demand signals, price ranges, competitors, suppliers, and possible sales channels. The aim is to give users useful areas to investigate before making decisions.

04

Build the app around the research flow

I worked on the interface and the steps that take a person's inputs through AI-assisted research and into a report. I want this process to feel straightforward instead of making users work through a complicated setup.

05

Connect the product pieces

I used my experience from PassSabi AI while working with GitHub, Supabase, Vercel, and AI integration. Building a second product has helped me practise connecting the interface, backend services, research logic, and deployment workflow.

06

Test, debug, and keep improving

The research and report-generation flow still needs careful testing. I'm working toward clearer steps, more readable reports, and results that communicate what needs further verification rather than making unsupported promises.

07

What I'm learning

Sabi Demand is teaching me that an AI product needs more than a good prompt. It needs useful inputs, a reliable workflow, understandable output, a clear interface, and repeated testing based on what actually breaks.
